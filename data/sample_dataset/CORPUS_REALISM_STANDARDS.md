# Corpus Realism Standards

**Companion to `PRODUCTION_READINESS_ROADMAP.md`** (that doc tracks *what/when*; this one governs *how* any future corpus or benchmark content should be authored). Established 2026-08-05, per explicit user direction. Applies to every future corpus-expansion or benchmark-addition task, not just one iteration.

## The litmus test

Before adding any ticket, interaction, or eval email, ask: **"Would a billing coordinator, practice manager, front-desk employee, insurance representative, or account manager realistically write this?"** If no, redesign it. Another engineer familiar with RCM should believe any given conversation could have come from a real customer inbox.

## The 12 dimensions

1. **Conversation realism** — ticke t lengths vary naturally (2-3 / 5 / 8-10 / 12-15 / 18-25 interactions), not uniform. Every interaction moves the business process forward; no padding ("still checking," "following up") unless genuinely warranted.
2. **Incoming email length distribution** — benchmark traffic should approximate 20% very short ("any update?", "thanks"), 30% short (2-5 lines), 30% medium (5-10 lines), 20% long (10-20+ meaningful lines). Long ≠ padded — every paragraph must carry real business content.
3. **Long-email variety** — long emails should span distinct communication purposes (initial issue, detailed follow-up, appeal, escalation, complaint, clarification, correction, status recap, informational update, internal findings, process question, resolution confirmation), not all be "detailed explanation."
4. **Natural conversation evolution** — within a single ticket, interaction length/tone should vary (long problem explanation → medium update → one-line follow-up → long appeal → medium update → "thanks"), not repeat one shape. Concretely, a genuinely long/deep ticket should draw from a real mix, not just 2-3 repeated shapes: long initial problem description, medium updates, one-line follow-ups ("Any update?"), attachment references, clarification questions, status recaps, customer corrections (of the agent's or their own earlier statement), payer responses, frustrated escalations where the timeline warrants it, thankful confirmations, short closing emails. Time gaps between interactions must also vary realistically within the same ticket — same-day exchanges, multi-day gaps, and week-plus silences — not a uniform interval. **Established 2026-08-05**: before expanding a new batch of tickets, produce one complete example ticket (every interaction, start to finish) for explicit approval first — it becomes the quality bar the rest of the batch is measured against, rather than each ticket being individually re-litigated.
5. **Writing style diversity** — professional, casual, friendly, busy-office-terse. Some typos/missing punctuation/lowercase/abbreviations/bullet lists — realistic, not overdone.
6. **Tone diversity** — polite, neutral, urgent, frustrated, confused, happy, thankful, concerned, impatient. Stored corpus currently has **zero** tone diversity (confirmed 2026-08-05: 0/93 customer-side interactions show any casual/frustrated/urgent marker) — this is the single largest gap against this standard.
7. **Formatting diversity** — plain paragraph, greeting+paragraphs, bullets, numbered questions, quoted/forwarded mail, reply chains, attachment references, signature blocks, varied spacing.
8. **Business workflow diversity** — every RCM service should have realistic alternative resolutions beyond denied→appeal→approved: claim corrected, eligibility updated, patient rescheduled, payment plan, coding correction, duplicate claim, partial payment, insurance investigation, provider clarification, coverage change, coordination of benefits, manual review, secondary payer. (Partial progress: Task 1's `P2`/`G2`/`E4` work already introduced unresolved-reconciliation, coding-correction, and COB shapes.)
9. **Sender diversity** — different roles (practice owner, billing coordinator, front desk, office manager, medical assistant, insurance rep, account manager) write differently. **Important technical split**: role/tone/signature diversity *within* a customer's existing single inbox address requires no pipeline change and can be done immediately. A literal *different sender email address* for the same customer depends on `PRODUCTION_READINESS_ROADMAP.md` Task 3 (customer-identification design discussion) — don't block the former on the latter.
10. **Business complexity tiers** — not every ticket should be equally hard. Some trivial, some moderate, some genuinely complex (multiple dates/claims/patients/documents/payer interactions, changing status, long delays, partial resolutions).
11. **Benchmark philosophy** — write a new eval case because it represents realistic production traffic, never because a taxonomy cell is empty.
12. **Production validation framework** — every corpus-expansion proposal must state: (1) why this happens in production, (2) which pipeline stages it exercises, (3) what production risk it reduces, (4) why it increases deploy confidence.

## Communication-pattern frequency tiers (added 2026-08-05)

Companion to `CORPUS_SCENARIO_COVERAGE_STRATEGY.md`, which maps *business* scenarios per RCM service. This section crosses that with *how* each scenario tends to actually arrive in an inbox — the same business capability should be exercised through more than one communication style, since that's what the pipeline actually receives as input, but not every scenario needs every style. Coverage should be frequency-weighted, not uniform:

- **Tier 1 — Universal** (should appear across most/all 6 services, both in stored ticket history and benchmark queries, because they occur regardless of the underlying business scenario): short follow-up / "Any update?", clarification question, status recap, thank-you/closure. These are cheap and high-value — prioritize spreading them widely.
- **Tier 2 — Common, scenario-weighted** (appear where the business scenario naturally invites them; aim for presence in most services, but don't force onto every ticket): long detailed/initial-issue explanation, attachment-referenced email (EOB/ERA/remittance/documentation).
- **Tier 3 — Situational, deliberately rare** (only where the specific scenario genuinely warrants it — a handful total across the *whole* corpus, not per-service, since manufacturing these into a calm routine ticket fails the litmus test): urgent request, frustrated escalation, manager/second-signer intervention. Concentrate these in scenarios that actually justify them (multi-week appeals, recurring systemic issues, compliance-sensitive refunds, genuine scheduling/deadline pressure) rather than spreading evenly for taxonomy's sake.

Applied so far: `G5`'s draft is the corpus's first example of Tier 3's manager-intervention pattern (Diane stepping in) — deliberately not repeated in the very next ticket, so it doesn't start feeling templated itself. `C7` (Stage 1, short/simple by design) should stay Tier-1-only — a routine POS-code fix doesn't warrant manufactured drama. `A5` (Stage 1, scheduling-pressure premise) is a natural home for Tier 3's "urgent request" specifically because the underlying scenario justifies it, not by assignment.

## Process (unchanged from the standing rule)

One reviewed batch at a time, full benchmark re-run after every corpus change, check existing eval cases referencing any touched ticket *before* writing content (the `C4`/`E2` and `E5`/`E4` incidents both came from skipping this), spot-check sibling tickets in the same customer pool afterward.
